How it works

ScamLens uses a Naive Bayes classifier. While the page loads, it counts how often each word appears in scam and in normal messages. Every word then gets a score for how much more it appears in scams. Add the scores up, and you get the verdict. Red words push toward scam, green words push toward safe.

Links, money amounts and numbers are replaced with placeholders so the model learns patterns, not specific values. Teaching it a message adds that message to its counts instantly.

Limits: the model trains on 48 short messages we wrote ourselves (synthetic, no real people’s data), so it is a small prototype. Accuracy is measured on 16 messages held out from training and can swing a lot with so little data. This is not financial advice. Never rely on it alone to decide whether to send money or click a link.
